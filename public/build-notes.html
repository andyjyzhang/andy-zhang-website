One stud scale.
A working spaceport.

The figures, rovers, furniture, machinery and architecture share one physical scale. One stud represents 8 mm; one plate is 3.2 mm; one brick is three plates tall. Large surfaces are assembled from standard rectangular pieces rather than stretched into invented bricks.

AssemblyParts used
Spaceport machineryStandard brick masts, round turntables, dishes, antennas, slopes and tiles; a flexible gantry cord is drawn as a line
VehiclesFour-stud bodies, 3641 tires, 4624 rims, 6157 wheel holders, 3787 mudguards, 3823 windscreen, 4079 seat and 3829 steering assembly
Buildings / streetsStandard 1×1 through 2×8 bricks; 1×1 through 16×16 plates; standard tiles including 8×16 pavement tiles
Suspended hull / propulsionStandard brick courses form armored beams and six octagonal engine nacelles. Transparent brick display cores and fixed exhaust courses connect to the engine caps with clear support rods. Animated light bands travel downward through the exhaust, without stretched cylinders or particle exhaust; reduced motion keeps their light steady.
Elevated expressA stepped brick-and-tile rail band follows rounded corners. Connected cantilever piers support the route; three independent carriages follow the same centerline.
Orbital gateBrick courses form a stepped annulus on a reinforced pedestal. Colored strips use bricks, without a smooth torus.

Choose Portfolio → Controls & settings → Download this build (.ldr) to export the placed catalog pieces at their actual model coordinates. Open the file in an LDraw-compatible LEGO CAD application with a current official parts library.

Printed signs and computer screens are custom sticker graphics on tile panels. Lit pieces represent transparent elements with optional lighting inside a display model. The stars are the background beyond the physical build.

For rendering, hidden underside tubes and covered studs are omitted from regular bricks and plates. The export uses the full catalog parts. Specialist molds retain their attributed LDraw surfaces. This is a digital assembly; a physical build or CAD connectivity and stability review is still needed before treating it as a verified construction kit. Every part/color combination has not been checked against retail inventory.
